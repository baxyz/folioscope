## How it works

<ol>
  <li>
    <span class="step">1</span>
    <strong>Your design file</strong>
    <p>A <code>.fig</code>, <code>.sketch</code>, <code>.xd</code> or <code>.penpot</code> file with a prototype in it: screens and the links between them.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <span class="step">2</span>
    <strong>On your page</strong>
    <p>One tag: <code>&lt;folioscope-player src="app.fig"&gt;</code>. It plays the file as it is, right away.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <span class="step">3</span>
    <strong>Baked for production</strong>
    <p>Bake it into a <code>.folio</code>: lighter, faster, and your source file stays private.</p>
  </li>
</ol>

<p class="formats">Plays files from <span>Figma</span><span>Sketch</span><span>Penpot</span><span>Adobe XD</span> · <a href="/compatibility">what plays, by format</a></p>

## Why “Folioscope”?

_Folioscope_ is the French word for a flip book: a stack of still pictures that come to life when you thumb through them. That is what this player does with your design files: it takes their still screens and flips through them following the links you designed, so the mockup moves again. And the “folio” is a nod to _portfolio_, where it all started.
